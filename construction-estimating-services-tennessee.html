Public work

Does Tennessee prevailing wage apply to your bid?

Usually only on road and bridge work. Tennessee’s Prevailing Wage Act (Tenn. Code Ann. § 12-4-401 et seq.) covers state-funded highway construction projects. The Tennessee Department of Labor and Workforce Development surveys highway contractors each year and publishes one statewide hourly rate per job classification; the current rates took effect January 1, 2026. The U.S. Department of Labor’s table of state prevailing wage laws notes that Tennessee has no state prevailing wage on non-highway construction.

Federal money changes that. The Davis-Bacon and Related Acts apply to federally funded or assisted contracts in excess of $2,000 for the construction, alteration or repair of public buildings or public works. Tell us which basis the bid documents call for — Tennessee highway rates, Davis-Bacon, union or open-shop labor — and we price to it. We do not decide whether a project is covered.

Bid day

Which Tennessee licensing rules affect a bid?

According to the Tennessee Department of Commerce and Insurance bidding FAQ, a contractor license is required before bidding, offering a price or contracting on projects of $25,000 or more, and a home improvement license is required from $3,000 where it applies. Under Tenn. Code Ann. § 62-6-119, the bid must show the prime contractor’s licensed name, license number, classification and expiration date, plus the same details for electrical, plumbing and HVAC subcontractors when their portion is $25,000 or more, and for masonry when that portion is over $100,000, materials and labor included.

We can show each of those trade portions as its own total, so you can see which subcontractors may need listing. The Board for Licensing Contractors governs the licenses themselves; checking them is your step, not ours.
